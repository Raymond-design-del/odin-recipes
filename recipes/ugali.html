Ugali

Description

Ugali is the quintessential Kenyan comfort food—a simple, wholesome cornmeal dish with a mild, nutty flavor and a dense, sliceable texture. Naturally vegan and gluten-free, it requires just two basic pantry ingredients and acts as the ultimate edible utensil alongside savory dishes like sukuma wiki (braised collard greens), beef stew, or nyama choma.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups (1 liter) water
  • 2 to 2 ½ cups (approx. 320 g) fine white maize meal (unga wa mahindi), plus extra for adjusting consistency
  • 1 pinch salt (optional)
  • • 1 tablespoon butter or margarine (optional, for extra richness)

Steps

  1. Boil the water. Pour the water into a heavy-bottomed pot (sufuria) over high heat and bring it to a full rolling boil. Stir in the salt and butter now if you are using them.

  2. Add the initial flour. Lower the heat to medium. Gradually pour in about half of the maize meal in a slow, steady stream while stirring continuously with a sturdy wooden spoon (mwiko) to prevent lumps.

  3. Thicken the mixture. Slowly add the remaining maize meal a little at a time while stirring vigorously until the mixture becomes thick and heavy.

  4. Knead and mash out lumps. Once the mixture is too stiff to stir easily, use the flat back of the mwiko to press, fold, and smash the dough firmly against the sides and bottom of the pot until completely smooth.

  5. Cook thoroughly. Reduce the heat to medium-low and continue turning and pressing the ugali for 5 to 8 minutes. It is fully cooked when it pulls cleanly away from the pot, smells like roasted corn, and holds a firm shape.

  6. Shape and serve. Gather the ugali into the center of the pot and pat it into a neat round dome using your wooden spoon. Turn off the heat and carefully invert the pot onto a serving plate so the ugali drops out cleanly. Serve immediately while hot.
