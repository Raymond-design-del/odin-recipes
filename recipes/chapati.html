Chapati

Description

Learn how to make the softest, most layered Kenyan chapatis from scratch! With a few easy rolling and folding tricks, you’ll get golden, flaky flatbreads that stay tender for hours.

Ingredients

(Makes 8 Chapatis)

  • All-purpose flour. Use 3 cups (about 375g) of plain wheat flour, plus a little extra for dusting your work surface. Do not use self-raising flour.
  • Warm or hot water. Use 1 to 1¼ cups (250–300ml) to bring the dough together and keep it soft.
  • Cooking oil or ghee. Use 2 to 3 tablespoons inside the dough, plus another 3 to 4 tablespoons for brushing the layers and pan-frying.
  • Salt. Use 1 teaspoon to balance the flavor.
  • Sugar. Use 1 teaspoon to 1 tablespoon for a subtle sweetness and golden-brown color.

Steps

  1. Mix and Knead the Dough
    • Combine the dry ingredients. Whisk the flour, salt, and sugar together in a large mixing bowl.Combine the dry ingredients. Whisk the flour, salt, and sugar together in a large mixing bowl.

    • Add oil and warm water. Stir 2 to 3 tablespoons of cooking oil into the flour, then gradually pour in the warm water while mixing with your hand until a soft, slightly sticky dough forms.

    • Knead until elastic. Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 8 to 10 minutes until it is completely smooth and springs back lightly when poked. Avoid adding too much extra flour, or the chapatis will turn out hard.

  2. First Rest
    • Coat and cover. Rub a thin layer of oil over the dough ball so it does not dry out.
    • Let the gluten relax. Cover the bowl with a damp kitchen towel or cling film and leave it at room temperature for 30 to 45 minutes.

  3. Coil for Layers (Second Rest)
    • Divide and flatten. Divide the rested dough into 8 equal balls, then roll one ball out into a very thin circle on a lightly floured counter.

    • Oil and roll into a rope. Brush the entire surface of the thin circle generously with cooking oil or melted ghee. Starting from one edge, roll the dough up tightly into a long, thin log.

    • Form a snail coil. Twist the log into a tight spiral (like a cinnamon roll or snail shell) and tuck the loose end underneath. Repeat with the remaining balls, cover them, and let the coils rest for 10 to 15 minutes.


  4. Final Roll Out
    • Flatten gently. Place one rested coil on a lightly floured surface and press it down with your palm.

    • Roll into a circle. Use a rolling pin to roll the coil outward from the center into an even circle about 2 to 3 mm thick (roughly 8 inches wide). Turn the dough a quarter-turn as you roll so it stays round without crushing the layers.

  5. Pan-Fry and Steam
    • Dry-cook the first side. Heat a heavy frying pan or tava over medium-high heat. Lay the rolled chapati on the dry pan for about 1 minute until small air bubbles appear on top and light brown spots form underneath.

    • Oil and brown both sides. Flip the chapati, brush the cooked top with a little oil, and flip it again. Gently press the surface with a spatula or folded cloth to help the layers puff up, then brush the other side with oil and cook until both sides have golden-brown spots.

    • Keep them soft. Transfer each cooked chapati into a hotpot or plate lined with a clean kitchen towel, covering the stack immediately so the trapped steam keeps them warm and pliable.
